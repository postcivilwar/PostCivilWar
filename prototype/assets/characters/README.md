# Characters

Every person in the game is the same base character: one head, one pair of
hands, one skeleton. What differs is what they wear, their hair, their build,
their height, their skin and small differences in the face.

## What is here

| Folder | What | Where it came from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `base/` | `streetwear_character_rigged.glb`: the base character, untouched. 43,130 triangles, 13 meshes, a 22-bone skeleton, one 4-second Idle clip. With the note and pictures that came with it | Supplied by the project owner. Its anatomy is the MakeHuman base mesh, CC0 (`MakeHuman_CC0_header.txt`). `SOURCE-README.md` is the note it arrived with |
| `clothing/` | 25 garments: T-shirt, polo, shirts, uniform, singlet, blouse, buba, senator, isi agu, kaftan, agbada, white coat, tunic, cassock, chasuble, dresses, boubou, trousers, shorts, wrappers, skirt | Generated by `tools/characters/wardrobe.py`, fitted to the base skeleton |
| `hair/` | 12: low cut, fade, afro, high-top, receded, bald on top, cornrows, braids, bun, puff, beard, goatee | Generated from the base head's own scalp and jaw (`headwear.py`) |
| `headwear/` | 10: head tie, scarf, gele, hijab, fila, hula, knitted cap, straw hat, hard hat, beret | Generated round the measured outline of the base head |
| `accessories/` | 11: beads, stethoscope, apron, bangles, watch, belt, sash, shoulder cloth, handbag, tie, reflective vest | Generated |
| `body/` | Bare arms, legs and feet, for what clothes leave showing | Generated. The base character has no body under its clothes |
| `footwear/` | Shoes, boots, sandals, slippers | Generated |

Each generated GLB is one skinned mesh on the base character's skeleton, bone
for bone, so it opens in Blender or Unreal and is worn on the same armature.
Each colour of cloth is its own material (`cloth_a`, `cloth_b`, ...); the game
gives them colours or prints when someone puts the part on.

No third-party model, texture or scan is used. Prints are painted in code.

## What the base character is, and what was done to it

- **One skeleton, 22 bones**: Root, Hips, Spine, Chest, Neck, Head; clavicle,
  upper arm, forearm and hand each side; thigh, shin, foot and toe each side.
  No fingers, no face bones. Y up, metres, facing +z, 1.82 m to the top of the cap.
- **The clothes are the body.** Under the hoodie and joggers there is nothing:
  the skin is a head, a neck and two hands. So the outfit cannot be taken off
  to show arms, and other clothes cannot be pulled over it. New clothes each
  bring the limbs they leave bare.
- **Bound standing wide.** Arms held out, forearms short (16 cm), feet 30 cm
  apart, to suit the big hoodie and shoes. Fitted clothes are cut for a second
  standing pose of the same bones, the *natural* pose: arms at the sides,
  forearm 23.5 cm, feet under the hips. Same bones, same names, same
  animations. The base character's own outfit is always worn in the pose it
  was bound in, so it looks exactly as supplied.
- **Skin weights are mostly rigid** (one bone a vertex). That is kept for the
  supplied parts. Generated parts blend over each joint.
- **The head had no eyes.** Behind the sunglasses the sockets and the inside of
  the mouth were empty pockets. The bake takes the pockets out, sets eyes in,
  and marks lips and brows as their own colours. The shape of the face is not
  changed.
- **Textures were flat colours**, apart from the hoodie's two-colour panel. The
  bake cuts the hoodie along the line between its colours, so everything is
  coloured per part and can be recoloured.
- **Idle only.** Walking, sitting, working and riding come from the game's own
  movements, applied to the same bones. The Idle clip (a breath and a slight
  shift, under 1.2 degrees) plays under them.

## Levels of detail

| Level | Used for | The base character | A typical townsperson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 full | the player; close-ups | 44,500 triangles, nothing thinned | about 20,000 |
| 1 near | within 7.5 m | 11,000 | about 7,900 |
| 2 middle | to 24 m | 4,000 | about 3,300 |

Beyond that, and when more than a couple of dozen people are close, people
are drawn as the simple instanced figures the crowd already used, in their
own colours.

## Rebuilding

```
python3 tools/characters/bake.py          # the kit the game loads: src/client/character-kit-data.js (about half a minute)
python3 tools/characters/export_glb.py    # the GLB files in this folder
```

Needs Python 3 with numpy, scipy and Pillow. Nothing is downloaded.

## Bringing in another asset

```
python3 -I tools/characters/inspect_asset.py path/to/new.glb
```

It reports size and orientation, weight, materials and textures, whether the
skeleton is the base character's, and whether it can be worn as it is. An
asset is added to the bake only once it passes; it never replaces the base
character. Something with no skeleton (a hat, a bag) is fixed to one bone.
Something skinned to a different skeleton needs its weights carried over to
this one first.
